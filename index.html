FIELD GUIDE · VERSION TWO

One atlas. Two kinds of map.

Map is always the opening view. 3D tilts the Earth–Moon system; drag to rotate it. Moon focuses on the lunar neighborhood. Mars pulls back to the interplanetary supply network.

Solid paths are model dynamics.

The halo, Lyapunov, distant retrograde and Arenstorf-type cycler examples are independently calculated numerical solutions of the circular restricted three-body problem (CR3BP). L4/L5 tracks are finite-time libration samples, not exact closed periodic orbits. The state vectors and equations are included in the source. Bodies are visually enlarged.

Dashed links are proposed logistics.

The Mars supply-network layout is deliberately compressed, like a metro map. Its curves represent candidate transport relationships, not solved flight trajectories. A line does not guarantee feasibility, a scheduled service or infrastructure that already exists. Capture-orbit nodes refer to rendezvous neighborhoods, not a unique orbit. Gale Crater is a reference destination, not a proposed construction commitment.

Solar orbits add physical context.

This inset uses circular coplanar Earth/Mars reference orbits and a Sun-centered Hohmann transfer. It is not a date-specific ephemeris. Transit time and initial phase angle are computed from the idealized model. Mars-local rings and surface links in the network are explanatory geometry.

Know the limits.

The CR3BP omits the Sun, eccentricity, mascons, non-gravitational forces and spacecraft maneuvers. It is an educational atlas, not flight-navigation software. Staging, refueling, resource extraction and settlements remain architecture concepts here. The original v1 source was not available: v2 is a new standalone implementation, not the original 71-record catalog.
