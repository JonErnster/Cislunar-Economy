# How Chang’e-6 communicated during its far-side landing

Earth–Moon–Mars Atlas · AI-assisted research note · 20 November 2026

Which links carried information between the Chang’e-6 lander and Earth during powered descent? Beijing Aerospace Control Center issued the descent command at 06:09 on 2 June 2024, Beijing time (UTC+8). The lander–ascender touched down at 06:23. In UTC, both events took place on **1 June, from 22:09 to 22:23**. The spacecraft used onboard guidance to avoid obstacles and select its final landing site. [Descent command][command] · [Landing and guidance][cnsa]

| Part of the path | What the mission record establishes | Evidence |

| Surface user | The Chang’e-6 lander–ascender descended to the Apollo basin on the lunar far side. The landing site had no direct line of sight to Earth. | [CNSA landing account][cnsa] · [Spacecraft team’s technical account][cast] |
| Orbital relay | Queqiao-2 relayed communications between Earth control and the spacecraft during descent. | [Spacecraft team’s technical account][cast] |
| Physical ground antenna | **Unknown for this contact.** The network builder identifies Jiamusi, Kashgar and stations in Argentina among the sites supporting Chang’e-6. Its account describes their combined coverage and leaves the individual dish or dishes used during descent unspecified. | [Network builder’s retrospective][cetc] |
| Flight control | Beijing Aerospace Control Center issued the command that began powered descent. This identifies the command origin. The radio link could terminate at a geographically separate antenna. | [CNSA descent chronology][command] |

ESA identifies two contacts that illustrate the importance of timing. Kourou received the spacecraft’s first signals after launch on 3 May. Maspalomas tracked its return to Earth on 25 June. Neither observation identifies the antenna used during the lunar landing. [ESA ground operations report][estrack]

## A comparison with Lunar Pathfinder

Queqiao-2 carried traffic for a named user during a dated event. ESA’s 12 October 2026 update describes a **planned** far-side relay for LuSEE-Night. Lunar Pathfinder was in final environmental testing, with launch planned for 2027. A future service record would need evidence that the deployed relay exchanged data with its intended user. [ESA mission update][esa]

The Chang’e-6 contact demonstrates a communications need for this mission. Estimating a shared relay’s break-even price would also require evidence of paying demand, usable contact hours, ground-station costs and operating costs. The event sources provide no customer price or utilization series from which to estimate it.
